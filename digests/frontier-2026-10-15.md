# Frontier watch — 2026-10-15

## Benchmarks & methods

- **[Similar Models, Broken Odds: Where Luce's Axiom Fails in Chatbot Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19095)** · 2026-10-15
  Shows Bradley-Terry ranking in Chatbot Arena systematically misjudges near-duplicate models, a concrete flaw in a widely-used LLM leaderboard methodology.
- **[Fantastic Metrics and How to Find Them: Meta-Evaluating Metrics for Multi-lingual Style-Personalized Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19166)** · 2026-10-15
  Proposes a regression-based meta-evaluation framework showing correlation-based metric/LLM-judge validation is unreliable, directly useful for designing better eval metrics.
- **[Tail Competition Explains Scaling in Best-of-N Sampling for Verifiable Problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19362)** · 2026-10-15
  Provides a theoretical account of why Best-of-N inference scaling works via extreme-value competition between correct/incorrect reward tails, relevant to RL/inference-time scaling and reward model limitations.
- **[Are We Really Measuring Forgetting? Pitfalls in Evaluating Machine Unlearning for Multimodal LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19613)** · 2026-10-15
  Identifies methodological pitfalls in how machine unlearning is measured for multimodal LLMs, relevant to rigorous eval design beyond just capability benchmarking.
- **[Sparse Autoencoders for Mechanistic Interpretability in NLP: A Critical Survey of Recoverability, Interpretability, and Utility](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20139)** · 2026-10-15
  A critical survey of 138 SAE studies assessing recoverability, interpretability, and utility claims, useful for calibrating confidence in SAE-based mechanistic interpretability methods.
- **[ListQA: A Benchmark for Evaluating List-Formatted Factual Knowledge Retrieval in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19165)** · 2026-10-15
  A new benchmark targeting list-formatted multi-fact retrieval, a factual knowledge eval dimension not covered by existing single-answer QA benchmarks and could be adopted broadly.
- **[Diagnosing and Correcting Reward Hacking in Verifiable Instruction Following](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19525)** · 2026-10-15
  Shows frontier models get full credit on verifiable instruction-following benchmarks despite substantively empty answers, demonstrating a concrete reward-hacking failure mode in verifier-based training/eval.
- **[$\tau$-Emotion-Bench: Exposing the Interaction Fragility of LLM Agents through Real-World User Emotions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19560)** · 2026-10-15
  Introduces a tool-calling agent benchmark that injects realistic user emotional variation to test interaction robustness, directly relevant to human-AI interaction and agent eval.
- **[Divergent Paths: Learning an Outcome-Predictive Representation of LLM Agent Trajectories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19614)** · 2026-10-15
  Shows agent rollout success/failure separate linearly in action-observation embedding space (up to 0.95 AUROC), a reusable signal for predicting and diagnosing agent task outcomes relevant to RL/reward-hacking analysis.
- **[Sparse Autoencoders Are a Shared Coordinate System for Symbolic Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19636)** · 2026-10-15
  Uses sparse autoencoder features as a shared coordinate system to ground neuro-symbolic matching, a concrete mechanistic interpretability application combining SAEs with symbolic reasoning.
- **[RM-OPD: On-Policy Distillation for Generative Reward Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20174)** · 2026-10-15
  Introduces on-policy distillation for generative reward models to fix reward sparsity and cross-domain interference in mixed-domain RLHF, directly relevant to reward modeling and reward hacking mitigation.
- **[Is This Just Fantasy? Language Model Representations Reflect Human Judgments of Event Plausibility](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12553)** · 2026-10-15
  Probes whether LM internal representations track human plausibility/modality judgments (possible vs impossible vs nonsensical), relevant to interpretability and model evaluation of semantic competence.
- **[Anticipatory Evaluation of Language Models: Predicting LLM Benchmark Scores from Descriptions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20645)** · 2026-10-15
  Introduces EVALCAST, a 2,290-example resource and method for predicting benchmark scores from task descriptions before running experiments, a new eval methodology to reduce evaluation costs.
- **[BTS-AgentBench: A Deterministic, Replayable Pipeline from Read-Only Telemetry Logs to Agent Benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27334)** · 2026-10-15
  Introduces a deterministic compiler turning raw operational telemetry logs into replayable agent benchmarks with source-verifiable answers, a reusable eval harness construction method for agentic LLM evaluation.
- **[The Instability of Safety: How Random Seeds and Temperature Expose Inconsistent LLM Refusal Behavior](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12066)** · 2026-10-15
  Shows LLM safety refusal decisions are highly unstable across random seeds and temperature in four instruction-tuned models, directly challenging the validity of standard single-shot safety eval protocols.
- **[Dual-Evolving Reinforcement Learning for Automated Reward Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13399)** · 2026-10-15
  Proposes a dual-loop RL framework where a proposer learns to design reward structures from its own trial outcomes, relevant to automated reward design and reward hacking research.
- **[CausalDS: Benchmarking Causal Reasoning in Data-Science Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08093)** · 2026-10-15
  A new benchmark testing causal reasoning in LLM-based data-science agents, filling a gap between symbolic causal benchmarks and realistic data analysis tasks, useful for agent capability evaluation.
- **[Rubric Dropout: A Simple Way to Mitigate Reward Hacking in Rubric-as-Reward RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11669)** · 2026-10-15
  Proposes rubric dropout to reduce reward hacking in rubric-as-reward RL training, directly addressing a core reward-hacking failure mode in LLM post-training.
- **[Mechanistic Circuit Identification for Controllable Data Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24065)** · 2026-10-15
  Introduces a mechanistic-interpretability circuit identification method to guide controllable data generation, linking circuit analysis to training-data valuation.
- **[IsoVRBench: Video Reward Modeling Without Human Feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17234)** · 2026-10-15
  Builds a controlled-intervention benchmark isolating individual video properties (alignment, fidelity, motion) to diagnose what video reward models actually discriminate, an eval methodology transferable to reward-model auditing.
- **[Beyond Successful Play: Diagnosing Opponent Modeling in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17310)** · 2026-10-15
  New benchmark/diagnostic framework testing whether LLM agents use genuine theory-of-mind vs. learned heuristics in repeated hide-and-seek against belief-modeling opponents.
- **[Evidence-First Agentic Verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17724)** · 2026-10-15
  Proposes evidence-grounded agentic verification to fix rubric judges that overlook evidence or accept unsupported self-reports, directly relevant to RL reward-hacking in agentic settings.
- **[DuplexAct: How Interactive Are Frontier Full-Duplex Spoken Language Models?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17738)** · 2026-10-15
  New benchmark for evaluating action-level interactivity (turn-taking, barge-in, etc.) in full-duplex spoken LLMs, filling gaps in existing human-AI interaction eval harnesses.
- **[Memory Echo in Single-Agent Long-Term Memory: Lifecycle Evaluation and Provenance-Guided Mitigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17763)** · 2026-10-15
  Introduces a lifecycle evaluation and provenance-based mitigation for 'memory echo' bias in agent long-term memory systems, relevant to human-AI interaction and agent evaluation methodology.
- **[Benchmarking and Training Reward Models for Cross-Source Evidence Grounding in Search Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17827)** · 2026-10-15
  Introduces a benchmark and trained reward models for evidence grounding/source credibility in search agents, directly relevant to reward modeling and reward hacking research.
