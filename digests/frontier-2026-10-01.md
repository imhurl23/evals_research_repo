# Frontier watch — 2026-10-01

## Benchmarks & methods

- **[CruxBench: A Benchmark of Information Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35879)** · 2026-10-01
  Introduces CruxBench, a benchmark testing whether LLMs can decompose complex problems into the right sub-questions ('cruxes') rather than just matching final answers, a new evaluation method for reasoning quality.
- **[SCOUT: Synergizing Reasoning and Tool-Use for Computer-Use Safety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36201)** · 2026-10-01
  Proposes SCOUT, a reasoning+tool-use verifier/eval harness for detecting subtle unintended harms from computer-use agents, directly relevant to agent safety evaluation.
- **[Population Fidelity: Evaluating Population Representativeness in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36253)** · 2026-10-01
  Introduces 'Population Fidelity,' an evaluation framework measuring whether LLM-simulated populations faithfully represent real human attitude distributions and subgroups, relevant to human-AI interaction evaluation.
- **[G\"odel Forest: Balancing Search Depth and Breadth for Data-Centric Recursive Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36675)** · 2026-10-01
  Proposes a search-depth/breadth balancing method for data-centric recursive self-improvement (training on agent-generated data), relevant to RL self-improvement and reward-hacking dynamics in model training loops.
- **[ProgressCompass: Embodied Progress Reward Models Are Lost Without the Right Context](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36684)** · 2026-10-01
  Shows embodied Progress Reward Models fail without richer context on long tasks, a concrete finding on reward model design relevant to RL reward shaping and hacking risks.
- **[Dating the Model: Hidden Dates in System Prompts Affect LLM Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36931)** · 2026-10-01
  Shows that undisclosed date-injection into system prompts causes measurable, uncontrolled day-to-day variation in LLM benchmark scores across MCQA, math, code, and MT, a reproducibility confound for eval researchers.
- **[Pair Difficulty Matters: Rethinking Pairwise LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluation and Consistency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37577)** · 2026-10-01
  Critically re-examines the standard position-bias/transitivity/agreement proxies used to validate LLM-as-judge systems, directly relevant to how researchers select and trust judge models.
- **[LongHarness Bench: Stress-Testing Language Model Harnesses for Long-Context Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38137)** · 2026-10-01
  Introduces a benchmark specifically designed to differentiate long-context LM harnesses on both effectiveness and efficiency where existing long-context evals have saturated.
- **[When Trees Are Not Enough: Learning Mixed-Topology Feature Graphs with Adaptive Graph Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36294)** · 2026-10-01
  Proposes Adaptive Graph SAEs that learn mixed-topology (multi-parent) feature graphs rather than tree/forest structures, advancing structured sparse autoencoder interpretability methods.
- **[Asking for What Was Never Requested: Horizontal and Vertical Proactivity in Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37236)** · 2026-10-01
  Introduces a new axis of agent proactivity (pursuing unstated but needed information) relevant to designing and evaluating human-AI interactive agents.
- **[Risk-Controlled Selective LLM Answering by Pricing Label-Free Checks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37493)** · 2026-10-01
  Introduces PriceCheck, a method for pricing label-free verification checks to control error rates in selective LLM answering/abstention, relevant to eval reliability research.
- **[When Models Don't Manipulate Manifolds: The Geometry of a Comparison Task](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37680)** · 2026-10-01
  Mechanistic interpretability study questioning whether low-dimensional manifold geometry actually explains model computation on a comparison task.
- **[TagPR: Tag-Guided Process Supervision for Personalization Reasoning in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23140)** · 2026-10-01
  Proposes tag-guided process supervision (RL) for personalization reasoning, addressing flawed reasoning chains despite correct outcomes—relevant to reward hacking/process supervision.
- **[On Calibration of Large Language Models: From Response To Capability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13540)** · 2026-10-01
  Distinguishes response-level vs capability-level calibration in LLMs, a new framing for confidence estimation evaluation methodology.
- **[RAWR: Reward Assignment Without Rollouts in Verifiable Domains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17815)** · 2026-10-01
  Introduces MCNIG, a rollout-free method for training process reward models in verifiable domains, directly relevant to RL reward modeling research.
- **[AdvancedMathBench: A Benchmark Suite for Advanced Mathematical Proof Generation and Verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11849)** · 2026-10-01
  New benchmark suite for advanced mathematical proof generation and verification with finer-grained evaluation than final-answer correctness.
- **[CultureConverse: A Multilingual Multi-turn Simulation Harness for Culturally Grounded Assistance in East and Southeast Asia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28405)** · 2026-10-01
  New multilingual multi-turn simulation harness evaluating culturally grounded assistance across East/Southeast Asian languages, a reusable eval methodology.
- **[Reproducing, Analyzing, and Detecting Reward Hacking in Rubric-Based Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04923)** · 2026-10-01
  Introduces a framework to reproduce and detect reward hacking in rubric-based RL with LLM judges, directly relevant to reward hacking research.
- **[Sense and Sensitivity: Benchmarking LLM Clinical Triage Recommendations with Physician Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38600)** · 2026-10-01
  A new benchmark (6,000 scenarios, 7,000 physician annotations) testing LLM robustness to text perturbations in clinical triage versus human experts, offering a transferable eval methodology for robustness testing.
- **[AgBench: Agentic AI Benchmarks for Personal AI Devices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38652)** · 2026-10-01
  A new benchmark for agentic AI performance and trade-offs on resource-constrained personal devices, relevant to agent evaluation harnesses.
- **[Talk2Agent: Benchmarking Voice Interfaces for Text Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38867)** · 2026-10-01
  A new benchmark evaluating how speech/ASR transcription errors affect computer-use agent task success, relevant to human-AI interaction and agent eval.
- **[MADBench: Benchmarking the Security of Multi-Agent Debate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39146)** · 2026-10-01
  A new benchmark testing adversarial robustness and error propagation in multi-agent debate systems, relevant to reward hacking and adversarial eval of LLM agents.
- **[Learning Process Rewards via Reasoning State Propagation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39220)** · 2026-10-01
  Proposes a method for training process reward models with reduced annotation cost by propagating outcome supervision to intermediate reasoning states, relevant to RL reward design and potential reward hacking vectors.
- **[On the Complexity of Preference-Based Bandits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39351)** · 2026-10-01
  Theoretical analysis of sample complexity for Bradley-Terry preference-based bandits with general function classes, directly underpinning RLHF/preference learning methodology.
- **[FIGS: Evaluating Multi-Turn Sycophancy Without Penalizing Empathy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39863)** · 2026-10-01
  Introduces a benchmark (FIGS) specifically designed to measure multi-turn sycophancy while distinguishing it from legitimate empathetic responses, a nuanced eval method for human-AI interaction research.
